SCORING SYSTEM

A detailed breakdown of every scoring category, why each rule exists, and how they work together to reward elite dynasty play.

THE PHILOSOPHY

This scoring system is built around one idea: reward players who do more things well. Standard PPR leagues make WRs and TEs too dominant because every catch scores the same regardless of impact. This system adds layers — yard bonuses, completion penalties, positional premiums — that force you to evaluate players the way NFL teams do.

The result is a Superflex TE Premium format where every position matters, QBs are premium assets, TEs that actually produce are worth investing in, and raw volume alone doesn't win championships. You need to build rosters like a GM, not just stack pass catchers.

Every scoring quirk here has a reason. Incomplete pass penalties keep QBs accountable. Rush attempt bonuses reward workhorse RBs. Yard-band reception bonuses separate route runners from possession receivers. Read on to understand why.

🏈
PASSING
Quarterback scoring · The premium position
Why QBs score so much here: In a Superflex league, you start 2 QBs. That means elite QBs are the most valuable asset on the board. The passing system rewards big-play ability (40+ yard completions, long TDs) while brutally penalizing turnover-prone QBs through pick-6 penalties and incompletion deductions. A gun-slinger who pads stats but loses games will actually score worse here than a careful, efficient QB.

RUSHING
Running back scoring · Workhorses win
Why rush attempts score points here: Standard leagues only reward yards and TDs, which means committee backs are nearly worthless. Adding 0.25 per rush attempt means a back who gets 20 carries regardless of yards is worth owning. Combined with 1st down bonuses and game-volume bonuses, true workhorse RBs become elite assets again — which is how it should be in dynasty.

RECEIVING
WR/TE/RB receiving · Yardage tiers separate the elite
Why the reception yard-band bonuses exist: PPR leagues reward volume catchers equally — a 3-yard dump-off scores the same as a 25-yard dig route. These yardage tier bonuses mean that YAC monsters, deep threats, and contested catch specialists score significantly more than screen-game dependent receivers. The TE premium (0.5 extra per reception) makes legitimate pass-catching TEs worth a premium pick, rewarding teams that invest there.

KICKING
Distance-based scoring · Range matters
Why kickers score this way: Rather than a flat score per FG, this system rewards range and penalizes missed makeable kicks. A kicker who drills a 55-yarder earns more than one who only converts chip shots. The miss penalties scale inversely — missing a 15-yarder is inexcusable (-5) while missing a 55-yarder loses you less (-1). This makes kicker selection a real decision, not an afterthought.

TEAM DEFENSE
DST scoring · Dominant defenses get rewarded
Why DST scoring is this detailed: Most leagues just use points allowed. This system layers in yardage allowed, individual defensive plays, and special situation bonuses so that a defense that dominates the game — shutting down drives, forcing turnovers, stopping short yardage — scores dramatically more than one that just gets lucky with a pick-6. You're incentivized to find defenses that are consistently dominant, not just boom/bust.

MISCELLANEOUS
Fumbles · Ball security matters
Why fumbles are penalized this heavily: Losing the ball is the worst thing a player can do. The dual penalty — -1 for fumbling and -2 for losing it — means a fumble lost costs 3 total points. This makes ball-secure RBs and QBs significantly more valuable than their fumble-prone counterparts with similar stat lines.
